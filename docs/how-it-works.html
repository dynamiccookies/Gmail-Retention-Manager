How it works

Gmail filters select. Retention Manager expires.

The application adds scheduled enforcement to labels you control. It does not apply a blanket retention policy to your mailbox.

  1. Create or use a retention labelA label below your configured root, such as Retention/7d or Retention/3m, represents a retention period.
  2. Choose which conversations receive itApply the label manually, in bulk, or through a Gmail filter. Unlabeled conversations are outside the retention process.
  3. Let the scheduled scan evaluate ageThe newest message in a conversation determines its age. A new reply resets the clock for the entire conversation.
  4. Apply the policy that keeps mail longestIf a conversation has multiple valid retention labels, the expiration date furthest in the future wins.
  5. Move expired active messages to TrashThe app moves eligible messages to Gmail Trash. Messages already in Trash are left there.
  6. Review the resultThe run status appears in the sidebar and administration page. Deletion summaries identify what was moved.

Schedules are checkpoints

A retention period does not promise deletion at an exact second. It becomes eligible after the period ends and is handled by the next successful scheduled scan. The application shows the expected trigger window because Apps Script schedules run within a range.

Calendar units stay calendar-aware

Minute, hour, day, and week policies use elapsed time. Month and year policies follow calendar dates. The saved schedule time zone is used consistently so travel or a browser time-zone change does not silently alter established processing.

Important: Retention policies apply to entire Gmail conversations. Always test labels and filters on noncritical email before expanding their use.
